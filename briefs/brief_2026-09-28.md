## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** coverage of Friday's New Mexico jury verdict against Meta led
with the ceiling number — "Meta could owe over $200 billion in penalties" (Fortune), "$219
billion verdict" (Vocal Media) — framing $219.5 billion as close to Meta's actual exposure.

**What the primary document actually says:** the jury's role was limited to finding liability
and counting violations — 43,899,720 of them under the state's Unfair Practices Act. The
$219.5 billion figure is simple arithmetic (43,899,720 × $5,000 statutory maximum per
violation), not a judgment. New Mexico's Department of Justice has said it will *seek* the
maximum; the actual civil penalty is a separate decision that District Judge Francis Mathew
will make at a hearing scheduled for October 1 (New Mexico Political Report, IBTimes UK).
Judges setting per-violation civil penalties routinely land well below a statutory ceiling,
particularly on a jury finding of non-willful conduct — and it bears noting Meta's own March
settlement in the related child-safety case was $942 million, several orders of magnitude
below that case's own theoretical maximum.

**The gap:** "$219.5 billion" is real math on a real violation count, so it isn't fabricated —
but presenting it as what Meta "could owe" collapses a three-step process (jury verdict →
maximum ceiling → judge's actual award) into one number, in the step of the process most
favorable to a dramatic headline. The one fact most headlines omitted or buried: there's a
specific hearing date, October 1, when the real number starts to take shape. **Label: FACT**
on the violation count, the $5,000 statutory maximum, and the math ($219.5B); **PREDICTION/my
read** that the eventual penalty lands meaningfully below the ceiling, based on the March
case's own ceiling-to-settlement ratio as the closest comparable.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** a statutory maximum and an expected judgment are two different
numbers, and headlines built for impact naturally lead with the bigger one. **Inference
(medium confidence):** Meta's stock dropping only 3.3–3.4% Friday — not double digits — is
itself evidence the market already understands this distinction better than the headlines
conveyed.

➡️ *What you could do:* When a "could owe $X billion" headline crosses citing a statutory
maximum, check two things before reacting: (1) is a maximum-per-violation calculation, not a
judgment, and (2) is there a scheduled hearing or ruling date — here, October 1 — that will
actually move the number. Both checks took under five minutes and materially changed how
alarming the headline should be treated.

### Glossary
- **Basis point (bp)** — 1/100th of a percentage point.
- **FOMC** — the Federal Open Market Committee, the Fed body that sets interest-rate policy.
  Next meeting October 27-28 (Section 10).
- **PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures) price index** — the Fed's preferred inflation
  gauge, released monthly by the Bureau of Economic Analysis. August data lands Wednesday,
  September 30, 8:30am ET (Section 11).
- **ISM Manufacturing PMI** — a monthly survey-based reading of US factory activity; above 50
  = expansion, below 50 = contraction. Due Thursday, October 1 (Section 11).
- **JOLTS** — the Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey, a monthly Bureau of Labor Statistics
  read on how many open jobs employers report; due Tuesday, September 29 (Section 11).
- **Strait of Hormuz** — the narrow shipping channel between Iran and Oman through which
  roughly a fifth of global oil trade passes; the subject of Iran's rejected reopening
  proposal (Section 4).
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the
  next 30 days. Below 16 = calm. Still 14.87, Friday's close (Section 3).
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish
  sentiment; the bull-bear spread (bulls minus bears) is the standard summary metric.
  Unchanged at −15.4; next update Thursday, October 1 (Section 3).
- **S-1** — the SEC registration statement a company files before an IPO; can be filed
  confidentially first before a public version appears on EDGAR. Anthropic's remains
  confidential (Section 10).
- **Statutory maximum penalty** — the highest fine a law allows per violation; a jury or
  regulator can find the number of violations, but a judge sets the actual per-violation
  amount, which is often well below the statutory ceiling (Sections 1, 9.5).
