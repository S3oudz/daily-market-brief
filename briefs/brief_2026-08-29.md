## 4. The story that matters most

**Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote pushed September rate-hike odds from roughly
35% to roughly 60% in a single day — even though, read in full, the speech explicitly declined to
commit to any rate path.**

**Fact — what moved.** CME FedWatch's implied probability of a quarter-point hike at the
September 15-16 FOMC meeting jumped to **roughly 59.7%** by late Friday morning, up from **35.4%
Thursday** and **39.9%** a week earlier. Gold fell as much as **1.7% to roughly $4,526/oz**
following the remarks; Bitcoin slid below $80,000 to a session low near **$76,846**; the
semiconductor and AI-infrastructure complex sold off broadly (Section 8).

**Fact — what Warsh actually said.** His most-quoted lines were about inflation ("running above
our 2 percent target... the Fed's predominant focus right now should be on prices"), but on
policy specifically he said: **"I stand here today committed to a discipline, not to a
decision."** He explicitly declined to offer a reaction function, calling that kind of forecasting
approach something that "works better in theory than in practice, better in the lab than in the
field," and said forward guidance "risks creating ambiguity in the name of clarity" — the opposite
of a committed hawkish signal. (Federal Reserve Board, full remarks:
[federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/warsh20260828a.htm))

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** the market moved roughly 20+ percentage points of implied hike
probability, and sold gold/crypto/high-multiple growth stocks accordingly, in response to a speech
whose most explicit policy statement was a refusal to commit to a path. **Inference (medium
confidence):** this is a case where the *tone* (concerned about inflation, skeptical of easing)
carried more market-moving weight than the *substance* (no new commitment) — see Section 9.5 for
the full headline-vs-primary-source breakdown, because this is exactly the kind of framing gap
this brief exists to catch.

➡️ *What you could do:* Treat Friday's ~60% September hike probability as a market inference from
tone, not a Fed commitment — the primary text leaves Warsh free to hold, cut, or hike depending on
data between now and September 16, so don't treat Friday's repricing as more settled than the
underlying speech actually was.

## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** Coverage of Friday's Jackson Hole speech led overwhelmingly with
"Warsh warns on inflation," framed the remarks as a clear hawkish signal, and tied the day's
selloff in gold, crypto, and small-caps directly to the Fed "signaling" a rate hike is likely in
September. Some outlets went further, describing Nvidia as having "barely budged" Friday — a claim
this brief checked and found false (Section 1: NVDA closed −4.57%, confirmed against an
independent price source after the claim looked inconsistent with the rest of the chip-sector
selloff).

**What the primary document actually says.** Warsh's own text, in the Federal Reserve's published
remarks, is explicit about *not* committing to a policy path: **"I stand here today committed to a
discipline, not to a decision."** He directly criticized reliance on a mechanical reaction function
("works better in theory than in practice, better in the lab than in the field") and said
traditional forward guidance "risks creating ambiguity in the name of clarity," saying he "much
prefer[s] another path." The inflation-is-too-high language was not new information — it restates
a position Warsh and the Fed have held for months. ([Federal Reserve Board, full remarks](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/warsh20260828a.htm))

**The gap.** The market moved as if Warsh had given fresh, directional guidance toward a September
hike (CME-implied odds jumped roughly 35% → 60%), when the text of the speech is more accurately
described as *reaffirming inflation concern while explicitly declining to guide on rates*. That
distinction matters for anyone using Friday's ~60% hike probability as a hardened market view: the
number reflects the market's *inference from tone*, not a Fed commitment, which leaves real room
for it to move again — in either direction — before the September 15-16 decision, especially if
incoming data (the September 3 JOLTs report, September 5 payrolls) shifts the picture.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** this is a case where the primary text directly contradicts the
dominant framing on the specific point that moved markets (whether Warsh committed to a rate path
— he explicitly said he didn't). **Inference (medium confidence):** a Fed chair who just told
markets "I'm not giving you my reaction function" and then watched the market immediately price a
25-point jump in hike odds off tone alone may see that gap as informative — it's a live, testable
question whether Warsh or other Fed officials push back on the ~60% pricing in the coming days if
they think markets over-read the speech.

➡️ *What you could do:* Treat the ~60% September hike probability as sentiment, not a settled
fact — watch for any Fed official (regional presidents speak frequently between now and the
meeting) explicitly confirming or walking back that pricing, since the primary text alone doesn't
support treating a hike as more likely than not.

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25 = stressed. Closed 14.43 Friday, essentially unchanged despite a rate-driven selloff in several asset classes (Section 3).
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish sentiment, published Thursdays. Historical averages: 37.5% bulls, 31.0% neutral, 31.5% bears. Latest (Aug 26/27): 32.9% bulls, 22.6% neutral, 44.4% bears; next reading Thursday, Sep 3.
- **CME FedWatch** — a tool that converts fed funds futures prices into an implied probability of the Fed's next rate move; jumped from ~35% to ~60% odds of a September hike after Friday's Warsh speech (Sections 4, 9.5).
- **Reaction function** — a description of how a central bank would respond to specific economic conditions (e.g., "raise rates if inflation exceeds X"); Warsh explicitly declined to offer one Friday, calling that kind of forecasting more reliable "in theory" than "in the field" (Section 9.5).
- **Forward guidance** — a central bank's public statements about its likely future policy path, intended to shape market expectations; Warsh said it "risks creating ambiguity in the name of clarity" (Section 9.5).
- **Gross margin** — the percentage of revenue left after direct production costs; Marvell guided Q3 gross margin down ~90bps on higher-cost custom-silicon production (Section 5).
- **Revenue-recognition timing** — when a company can count revenue as "earned" under accounting rules, distinct from when a deal is signed or its total size is known; the core of Friday's Marvell story (Section 5).
- **SMH** — the main semiconductor ETF and standard benchmark for chip-sector performance. Closed −3.44% Friday, in line with the sector-wide selloff (Section 9).
- **Falsifiable version** — this brief's standing discipline for speculative threads: state in advance what specific, checkable outcome would confirm or refute the read, so the next day's Scorecard has something objective to grade.
- **Jackson Hole** — the Kansas City Fed's annual economic policy symposium, August 27-29, 2026. Chair Kevin Warsh delivered his first keynote there Friday, August 28, 10:00am ET.
