💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** three distinct groups moved in three distinct directions on one

confidence):** this is markets doing real work, not blanket panic — investors are making a
specific bet about *who* absorbs the cost if AI capability growth paces slower (the picks-and-

vendors who'd sell more regardless).

➡️ *What you could do:* Stop treating "AI trade" as one basket. Monday drew a real line between
AI infrastructure suppliers (chips, power, cooling, optics — exposed to a capex slowdown) and AI
platform owners plus AI-adjacent security plays (insulated or benefiting) — that distinction is

## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** Coverage across CNBC, Yahoo Finance, and Fortune framed Monday's
selloff uniformly as "AI safety warnings" crushing chip and AI-infra stocks — treating the
Amodei/Altman/Musk pacing comments as the demand-side explanation for Broadcom's −4.72% and
similar moves across the complex.

**What the primary document actually says:** Broadcom's own Q3 FY2026 earnings call (September
2, before the weekend's essay) raised full-year AI revenue guidance to **$58 billion** (up from
prior guidance of $56 billion) and specifically flagged **accelerating** Q4 shipments — ramping
Ironwood to **Anthropic**, high-volume TPU v8i shipments to Google, continued Jalapeno shipments
to OpenAI, and new production shipments of Meta's custom MTIA chip (Motley Fool transcript,
Futurum, TechTimes). Broadcom itself is forecasting AI revenue to roughly double to $115 billion
in 2027 and double again to ~$230 billion in 2028 — guidance issued *after* the weekend's essay
had time to be reflected, and it went up, not down.

**The gap:** the coverage attributes Monday's chip-sector selloff to fears of an AI capex
slowdown, but the most AI-capex-exposed supplier in the sector (Broadcom, whose custom silicon
directly reflects what Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, and Meta are actually ordering) has guidance
pointing the opposite direction — accelerating, not pacing. If any of those four companies had
quietly cut a chip order, Broadcom's own numbers would be the first place it would show up, and
as of the most recent call, it hasn't.

**Second divergence — oil:** headlines this week (CNBC's "As the oil surge continues...") frame
Brent's move as an ongoing surge. The EIA's own September 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook,
published the same week, still forecasts Brent averaging **~$90/barrel in H2 2026** and falling
to **$77/barrel by Q2 2027** — the government's own primary supply/demand model expects this
spike to fade as Middle East export flows and shut-in production gradually recover, even after
raising its near-term estimate $8/barrel for the Hormuz disruption (EIA, Rigzone).

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** there's a real, checkable distance between "chip suppliers are
selling off on AI-pacing fears" and "the actual order data from those suppliers shows any
pacing" — the former is priced; the latter, per Broadcom's own guidance, hasn't happened.
**Inference (medium confidence):** Monday's chip-sector selloff may be trading on an anticipated
future slowdown that current order books don't yet show — which means it's a sentiment/valuation
reset, not (yet) a fundamentals-driven one.

➡️ *What you could do:* Watch Broadcom's, Nvidia's, and Micron's next earnings calls (AVGO
reports again in December, NVDA in November) for whether *actual* order guidance changes — that's
the fact that would convert Monday's move from "sentiment repricing" to "confirmed demand
destruction." Until then, the gap between the headline framing and the primary order data is the
thing to track.

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the
  next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25 = stressed. Jumped to 17.1 Monday (Section 3).
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish sentiment,
  published Thursdays. This week's reading (38.0% bulls / 39.3% bears) won't update again until
  Thursday, September 17 (Section 3).
- **FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee)** — the Fed's rate-setting body; 12 voting members. Meets
  September 15-16, with the decision and press conference both Wednesday afternoon, delivered by
  Chair Kevin Warsh (Section 1, 10, 11).
- **East-West Pipeline** — Saudi Arabia's main crude pipeline (7 million b/d capacity) that
  bypasses the Strait of Hormuz entirely, running from the Eastern Province to the Red Sea; its
  shutdown after drone strikes removed a key alternative to the strait, driving Brent's spike to
  $107.82 (Section 7).
- **"Pacing the frontier"** — the phrase from Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's September 2026 essay
  calling for slower AI *capability* improvement (not a halt to spending or training) to let
  safety/alignment work catch up (Section 4, 9.5).
- **Custom AI accelerator / ASIC** — a chip designed for one company's specific AI workload (e.g.,
  Google's TPU, Anthropic's Ironwood via Broadcom), as opposed to general-purpose GPUs like
  Nvidia's — Broadcom's guidance on these chips is a direct proxy for hyperscaler AI capex
  (Section 9.5).
- **S-1** — the SEC registration statement a company must file before an IPO. Anthropic
  confidentially filed one June 1, 2026; reporting still points to a public filing by end of
  September, unfiled as of this morning's direct EDGAR check (Section 10, 11).
- **Falsifiable version** — this brief's standing discipline for speculative threads: state in
  advance what specific, checkable outcome would confirm or refute the read, then grade it
  honestly once data exists (Section 10).
