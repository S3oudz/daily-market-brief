### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the
  next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25 = stressed. At 15.72 after two straight daily increases
  (Section 3).
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish sentiment,
  published Thursdays. Latest confirmed reading (week ending Sept 2): 39.7% bulls, 22.7% neutral,
  37.6% bears; next print due Thursday, September 10 (Section 3).
- **Brent vs. WTI** — Brent is the global oil benchmark (more exposed to Middle East/Strait of
  Hormuz risk); WTI is the US domestic benchmark. Both are now rising together ($99.49 / $94.63),
  a change from the Brent-led divergence seen earlier in the week (Section 7).
- **Strait of Hormuz** — the narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which a large share of
  the world's seaborne oil trade passes; site of the ongoing US-Iran tanker exchanges (Section 4).
- **"Tanker for tanker"** — a US retaliation policy reportedly approved by President Trump,
  striking Iranian oil tankers directly in response to Iranian tanker-mining incidents (Section
  10, Thread One).
- **S-1** — the SEC registration statement a company must file before an IPO, containing audited
  financials and legally binding disclosures. Anthropic confidentially filed one June 1, 2026; a
  direct EDGAR search confirmed for a fourth straight day that no public version exists
  (Section 5).
- **FOMC quiet period** — the informal, several-week window before each Federal Reserve meeting
  during which officials avoid public commentary on monetary policy; runs through the September
  16 decision (Section 11).
- **SMH** — the main semiconductor ETF and standard benchmark for chip-sector performance. Closed
  +1.20% today, the only green equity ETF in this brief's basket (Section 9).
- **Falsifiable version** — this brief's standing discipline for speculative threads: state in
  advance what specific, checkable outcome would confirm or refute the read, then grade it
  honestly once data exists (Section 10).

## What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** The dominant framing across wire coverage — including the very
Reuters headline surfaced in this brief's own news feed, "Oil jumps $1 in early trade after Iran
launches missiles at Jordan" — leads with Iran's missile attack as the standalone triggering
event.

**What the fuller reporting actually says:** Multiple outlets covering the same 24 hours (Al
Jazeera: "US attacks 5 Iranian oil tankers, Iran retaliates with attacks on Jordan"; a Reuters
report cited via Axios/CNN) establish that the US struck first in this specific exchange,
destroying five Iranian oil tankers under a reported new "tanker for tanker" retaliation policy
approved by President Trump — and Iran's Jordan strike is explicitly described as retaliatory for
that US action.

**The gap:** A headline that leads with "Iran launches missiles" without the immediately
preceding US tanker strikes in the same sentence gives the impression of unprovoked Iranian
escalation, when the fuller sequence — visible in the body text of the same and other wire
reports — is a US strike followed by an Iranian retaliatory strike. This matters for how a reader
assesses which side is escalating the underlying conflict, since headline-only readers get a
meaningfully different sequence of events than readers of the full story. This is a framing/
omission observation about headline structure, not a claim about intent.
