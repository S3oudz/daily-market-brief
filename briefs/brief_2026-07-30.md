## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** Several outlets covering Meta's earnings led with the capex number
as the story — one widely-circulated framing was "Meta's stock sinks as earnings disappoint and
capex guidance balloons to $145 billion," implying AI infrastructure spending itself had jumped
to a dramatic new level and was the primary driver of the after-hours selloff.

**What the primary document actually shows:** Meta's own Q2 2026 earnings release
([investor.atmeta.com](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx))
states full-year 2026 capex guidance moved from **$125–145 billion to $130–145 billion** — the
**$145B ceiling is completely unchanged** from the guidance the company already gave last
quarter; only the *floor* rose by $5 billion. The release also states plainly that the EPS miss
($6.18 versus $7.13–7.14 expected) came almost entirely from two one-time items: a $2.4 billion
charge for unspecified legal proceedings and a $1.2 billion severance charge tied to a May 2026
headcount reduction. Revenue beat estimates by over a billion dollars ($60.8B vs. $59.5B), and
excluding the two one-time charges, Meta would have beaten EPS estimates, not missed them.

**The gap:** "capex balloons to $145 billion" reads as a new, escalating number — but $145B was
already public information as of last quarter's guidance; the only change was a $5B narrowing of
the low end. The more accurate and more checkable story — that the earnings miss was driven by a
legal charge and a severance charge, not by operating deterioration or a capex shock — is
present in the primary release and was reported accurately by AP/ABC News, but the more dramatic
"capex ballooning" framing carried further in headline placement and circulation.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** this is a framing-emphasis gap, not a factual error — the $130–145B
number itself is accurate, and no outlet fabricated it. **Inference (medium confidence):** in a
week where "AI capex fear" is already the dominant market narrative (Alphabet's capex guidance
triggered a 7% drop just last week per the prior brief's own reporting), a genuinely ambiguous
number like "guidance range moved from $125–145B to $130–145B" gets pulled toward the scarier
available narrative almost automatically, even when the underlying data supports a more mundane
explanation (one-time charges) sitting right in the same press release.

➡️ *What you could do:* When a capex or spending "guidance increase" headline drives a stock
story, check whether the reported number is the *ceiling* or the *floor* of a range before
treating it as new information — Meta's actual new information today was a $5B floor increase
and $3.6B of one-time charges, not a step-change in AI spending plans.

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; expected S&P volatility. Low (<16) = calm; high (>25) = stressed.
- **AAII survey** — weekly poll of retail investors on whether they're bullish/bearish for the next 6 months.
- **PCE deflator (Personal Consumption Expenditures)** — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, distinct from CPI; tracked closely around FOMC meetings.
- **FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee)** — the Fed committee that sets interest rate policy; meets roughly every six weeks.
- **Dissent (FOMC)** — when a voting Fed official formally votes against the majority decision; a rare, closely watched signal of internal disagreement on policy direction.
- **DRAM (Dynamic Random-Access Memory)** — general-purpose computer memory; distinct from the specialized HBM chips used in AI accelerators, though made by the same companies (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, CXMT).
- **Capex (capital expenditures)** — money a company spends building long-term assets (here, AI data centers); a rising capex guidance range signals more planned spending, not necessarily worse near-term profitability.
- **ETF proxy** — an exchange-traded fund used to represent an index or asset (SPY for the S&P, GLD for gold); ETF proxies only trade during US market hours, unlike crypto or oil futures.
- **SOX / SMH** — the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and its tracking ETF (SMH); the standard benchmark for chip-sector performance.
- **Brent vs. WTI** — the two main global oil benchmarks; Brent is the international reference price, WTI is the US domestic one. They usually move together but can diverge on regional supply/demand shocks.
- **Basis point / bp** — one-hundredth of a percentage point; a 25bp move is 0.25%, the Fed's standard rate-adjustment increment.
