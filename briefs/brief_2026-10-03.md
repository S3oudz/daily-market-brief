## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** Friday's coverage led overwhelmingly with the headline miss —
"payrolls up just 29,000," "jobs report badly misses forecasts," "labor market falters" — framed
primarily around what it means for the Fed's October decision (CNBC, Yahoo Finance, multiple
outlets, via search).

**What the primary document actually says:** Buried in the same Bureau of Labor Statistics
release (bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm) are revisions showing the slowdown didn't start in
September. **Fact:** July's payroll gain was revised from an originally reported +21,000 down to
**−10,000** — a negative month — and August was revised down from +162,000 to **+133,000**.
Combined, the two revisions cut 60,000 jobs from what was previously reported as already-booked
employment growth (BLS, Yahoo Finance, via search).

**The gap:** A reader who only saw Friday's "September missed" headlines would reasonably
conclude this is a one-month surprise. The primary release says the opposite: July was already
negative before anyone noticed, and August was nearly 30,000 weaker than first reported — meaning
the labor market has now posted three consecutive disappointing prints (a negative month, a
downward-revised month, and September's badly-missed month), not one. **Label: FACT** on the BLS
revision numbers themselves (July −10,000, August +133,000, combined −60,000) and on the
September headline print (+29,000); **PREDICTION/my read** that a three-month pattern of
deceleration is a meaningfully different, more serious signal for the Fed and for markets than a
single soft month — and one that most of Friday's "one data point" framing didn't carry.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** the labor market has been weaker for longer than the September
headline alone suggests. **Inference (medium confidence):** this raises the stakes for the
October 7 FOMC minutes and the next payrolls report (early November) — if November's report also
disappoints, "the Fed is behind the curve" (the dovish-odds case that already won Friday's market
reaction, Sections 1, 4) becomes a three-month trend rather than a one-month scare, which is a
harder case for the Fed to wave off at its October 27–28 meeting.

➡️ *What you could do:* When next month's jobs report headline runs, check the same BLS release
for prior-month revisions before reacting to the headline number alone — this week's big story
was arguably the revisions, not the print, and revisions get far less coverage than the number
everyone was waiting for.

- **Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) / "dot plot"** — the Fed's quarterly chart of each
  policymaker's individual rate projection; distinct from market-implied hike odds (CME
  FedWatch), which reprice daily on new data and moved far faster than the dot plot this week
  (Sections 4, 9.5, 10).
- **CME FedWatch** — a tool that converts fed-funds futures prices into implied probabilities of
  a rate move at the next FOMC meeting; showed October-hike odds falling from ~36% to ~17–18%
  after Friday's jobs report (Section 4).
- **Nonfarm payrolls / payroll revisions** — the BLS's monthly count of jobs added outside
  farming; each month's release also revises the prior two months' figures as more complete data
  comes in. This month's revisions cut 60,000 jobs from July and August's previously reported
  totals (Section 9.5).
- **ADP National Employment Report** — a private payrolls estimate released ahead of the
  government's official nonfarm payrolls report; the two frequently diverge (Section 11).
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the
  next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25–30 = genuine stress. Currently 15.31 (Section 3).
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish sentiment;
  the bull-bear spread (bulls minus bears) is the standard summary metric. Unchanged at −11.9;
  next reading Thursday, October 8 (Sections 1, 3).
- **WTI vs. Brent crude** — two different crude oil benchmarks; WTI is the US domestic
  benchmark, Brent is the international (North Sea) benchmark (Section 7).
- **Put option** — a contract giving the holder the right to sell a stock at a set price; a way
  to bet a stock falls while capping the maximum loss to the premium paid (Section 10).
