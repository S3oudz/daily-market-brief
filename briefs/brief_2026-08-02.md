## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** Saturday's coverage (Bloomberg, Daily News Egypt, The Edge Singapore)
framed the LNG-tanker strike as fresh news driving an oil-price "rally" — headlines centered on
the single event and its immediate price reaction.

**What the primary chronology actually shows:** Per Wikipedia's maintained "2026 Strait of Hormuz
crisis" timeline and CSIS's tracker, this is not an isolated incident — the strait has been under
effectively continuous attack conditions since Iran declared it "closed" on March 4, 2026, with
documented incidents including missile/projectile attacks on commercial ships as early as March
11–15, and another Iran-attributed strike on July 7 that also prompted a Brent rally (per CNBC's
coverage of that date). Traffic through the strait collapsed to as few as two tankers a day at the
height of the initial war and has only partially recovered via a system of paid, pre-approved
transit routes.

**The gap:** Framing each new incident as "fresh escalation drives rally" is not wrong, but it
under-communicates that markets have now repriced roughly the same category of event at least
three times since March — the March attacks, the July 7 strike, and this weekend's LNG-tanker
strike. What's genuinely new about *this* incident is the target type (LNG rather than oil cargo,
Section 4), not the fact of an attack occurring, which the primary chronology shows is now a
recurring feature of a five-month-old crisis rather than a surprise. Readers reacting to "another
attack = another leg up" without noting the LNG-specific angle may be pricing the wrong dimension
of the news.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** this is at least the third notable Hormuz attack-and-oil-rally cycle
documented since March 2026. **Inference (medium confidence):** each cycle's price impact may be
getting smaller in relative terms as the market prices in an ongoing-crisis base rate (Brent's
1.2% move here is smaller than some earlier reaction), which would mean the market is starting to
treat "another Hormuz incident" as expected background risk — making the LNG-specific angle the
more informative piece of this particular headline, not the attack itself.

➡️ *What you could do:* When Hormuz headlines recur (and per the chronology, they likely will),
check what's different about the specific incident — cargo type, location, casualties, which
country claims responsibility — rather than reacting to the "another attack" framing alone, since
the base rate of attacks is now high enough that the framing itself carries less information than
it did in March.

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; expected S&P volatility. Low (<16) = calm; high (>25) = stressed.
- **AAII survey** — weekly poll of retail investors on whether they're bullish/bearish for the next 6 months.
- **FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee)** — the Fed's rate-setting committee; votes on the benchmark federal funds rate at each meeting, and dissents (votes against the majority decision) are a signal of internal disagreement about the right policy path.
- **10-year / 30-year Treasury yield** — the interest rate the US government pays to borrow over those terms; rising yields raise borrowing costs economy-wide and increase the discount rate applied to future corporate earnings, which pressures high-multiple growth stocks most.
- **Brent vs. WTI** — the two main global oil benchmarks; Brent is the international reference price, WTI is the US domestic one. They usually move together but can diverge on regional supply/demand shocks.
- **REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust)** — a company that owns income-producing real estate (here, data centers); REITs are typically rate-sensitive because their valuations depend on discounting long-dated rental cash flows.
- **ETF proxy** — an exchange-traded fund used to represent an index or asset (SPY for the S&P, GLD for gold); ETF proxies only trade during US market hours, unlike crypto or oil futures.
- **SOX / SMH** — the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and its tracking ETF (SMH); the standard benchmark for chip-sector performance.
- **Sandboxed testing environment** — an isolated, controlled space used to test AI models without giving them real-world internet or system access; "escaping" one means the AI accessed systems outside its intended containment.
- **Contract expiry (futures)** — futures contracts (e.g., oil) have fixed expiration dates; when one month's contract expires, trading rolls to the next month's contract, which can trade at a different price (this brief notes both September's $90.12 expiry and October's $87.93 as separate, non-comparable prices).
- **Basis point / bp** — one-hundredth of a percentage point; a 25bp move is 0.25%, the Fed's standard rate-adjustment increment.
