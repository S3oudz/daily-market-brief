### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; expected S&P volatility. Low (<16) = calm; high (>25) = stressed.
- **AAII survey** — weekly poll of retail investors on whether they're bullish/bearish for the next 6 months.
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)** — measures what consumers pay for goods/services; the headline inflation number markets watch most closely.
- **PPI (Producer Price Index)** — measures what producers/wholesalers pay before goods reach consumers; often a leading indicator for future CPI.
- **Capex (capital expenditure)** — money a company spends building or buying long-term assets (e.g., data centers, chips); heavy AI capex means big upfront spend before revenue catches up.
- **Free cash flow (FCF)** — cash a company generates after capital spending; negative FCF means the company is burning cash even if it's profitable on paper.
- **FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee)** — the Fed committee that sets interest rate policy; meets roughly every six weeks.
- **Blackout period** — the window before an FOMC meeting when Fed officials don't give public policy commentary, making the meeting itself the only signal.
- **ETF proxy** — an exchange-traded fund used to represent an index or asset (SPY for the S&P, GLD for gold); note ETF proxies only trade during US market hours, unlike crypto.
- **Sympathy move** — when a stock moves (usually down) because a related company in the same sector reported bad news, even without its own new information.
