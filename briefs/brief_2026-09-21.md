**The gap:** "soaring" is doing a lot of work to describe a sub-1% move. The underlying fact — oil
gapped up and held rather than fading, confirming markets treated the Riyadh strike as real news
worth a price move — is genuinely reportable and consistent with this brief's own Section 1 grading.
But the adjective inflates a modest, arguably reassuring data point (the market did *not* price in a
major supply disruption) into a dramatic one. Readers skimming headlines alone would likely come away
with a bigger view of the oil-market reaction than the number supports.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** this is a framing gap, not a factual error — every number cited in the
"soaring" headlines is accurate; only the adjective oversells it. **Inference (medium confidence):**
geopolitical-attack headlines attract more attention with dramatic language regardless of the
underlying number's size, which is exactly why this brief checks the futures print directly (Section
4, 7) rather than taking headline framing as a proxy for market reaction.

➡️ *What you could do:* When a headline uses words like "soaring," "surging," or "plunging" for a

### Glossary
- **Secondary offering (in an IPO)** — shares sold by existing shareholders (here, Bamboo's
  private-equity backers CVC and White Mountains), as opposed to a primary offering, where the
  company itself issues new shares and receives the proceeds. A fully secondary IPO raises no new
  capital for the company (Section 5).
- **Globex / ICE futures** — CME's and ICE's electronic trading platforms for oil and other futures,
  which trade Sunday evening through Friday afternoon (US time) nearly continuously — unlike US cash
  equities, which only trade during defined daytime hours. This is why a real oil futures print
  existed hours before this brief, while equity prices did not (Sections 2, 4, 7).
