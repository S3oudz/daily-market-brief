## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** Thursday's GDP release was widely covered as a growth
disappointment — CNBC's own headline read "U.S. economy slowed to 1.5% growth rate in Q2,"
framing the miss against the 1.8% consensus and the deceleration from Q1's 2.1% as evidence the
economy is losing momentum.

**What the primary data actually shows:** The Bureau of Economic Analysis's advance Q2 GDP
release ([bea.gov](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-2nd-quarter-2026)) shows the
1.5% headline miss was mechanically driven by a surge in imports — specifically capital goods
like telecommunications equipment and semiconductors, reflecting a rush of AI-infrastructure
purchases. Imports subtract from the GDP calculation by construction, regardless of whether the
underlying economic activity is healthy. The same release shows real final sales to domestic
purchasers — the measure that strips out trade and inventory swings to isolate actual household,
business, and government spending — rose at a 3.9% annualized pace, more than double Q1's 1.7% and
the strongest reading in over a year. Personal spending also accelerated, up 2.1% after a 0.4%
gain in Q1.

**The gap:** "GDP slowed to 1.5%, missing expectations" reads as broad economic weakening — but
the report's own underlying-demand measure shows the opposite: domestic spending accelerated
sharply, and the miss came almost entirely from companies importing more AI hardware (a bullish
signal for AI capex, not a recessionary one). The headline number is technically accurate, but the
"slowdown" framing inverts what the report's more informative internals actually say.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** this is a case where the standard GDP headline metric and the
report's own better demand-signal (final sales to domestic purchasers) point in opposite
directions, and only the weaker-looking number made most headlines. **Inference (medium
confidence):** given that the same report explains the "drag" as AI-related import spending, this
GDP release is arguably a *third* data point (alongside Microsoft's Azure numbers and Samsung's
memory-shortage warning) supporting Thursday's AI-capex-is-real market read — it just required
reading past the headline to see it.

➡️ *What you could do:* When a GDP miss or beat headline moves markets, check whether "final sales
to domestic purchasers" moved the same direction as the headline number before treating it as
confirmed economic weakness or strength — today, they moved in opposite directions, and the
internals were the more useful number for judging where the economy actually stands.

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; expected S&P volatility. Low (<16) = calm; high (>25) = stressed.
- **AAII survey** — weekly poll of retail investors on whether they're bullish/bearish for the next 6 months.
- **PCE deflator (Personal Consumption Expenditures)** — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, distinct from CPI; tracked closely around FOMC meetings.
- **Final sales to domestic purchasers** — a GDP sub-measure that strips out trade and inventory swings to isolate real household/business/government spending; often a cleaner read on underlying demand than the headline GDP number.
- **DRAM / NAND** — the two main categories of computer memory chips; DRAM is general-purpose working memory, NAND is used in storage (SSDs). Both are currently in a supply shortage driven by AI data-center demand.
- **Capex (capital expenditures)** — money a company spends building long-term assets (here, AI data centers); a rising capex guidance range signals more planned spending, not necessarily worse near-term profitability.
- **ETF proxy** — an exchange-traded fund used to represent an index or asset (SPY for the S&P, GLD for gold); ETF proxies only trade during US market hours, unlike crypto or oil futures.
- **SOX / SMH** — the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and its tracking ETF (SMH); the standard benchmark for chip-sector performance.
- **Brent vs. WTI** — the two main global oil benchmarks; Brent is the international reference price, WTI is the US domestic one. They usually move together but can diverge on regional supply/demand shocks.
- **SPAC (Special Purpose Acquisition Company)** — a blank-check shell company that raises money via IPO with the intent to later merge with a private company, taking it public; distinct from a traditional IPO where the operating company itself lists directly.
- **Basis point / bp** — one-hundredth of a percentage point; a 25bp move is 0.25%, the Fed's standard rate-adjustment increment.
