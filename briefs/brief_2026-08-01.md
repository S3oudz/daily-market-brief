## 4. The story that matters most

**Treasury yields spiked Friday on a fresh, and arguably underappreciated, Fed rate-hike scare —
and equities rallied through it anyway.** The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 4.75% and the
30-year at 5.25%, its highest level since 2007, after Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack and
Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari — two of the three officials who dissented from Wednesday's
hold decision in favor of a hike — said publicly on Friday that acting now against inflation is
necessary, with Hammack stating "now is the time for the Federal Open Market Committee to act to
speed the return of PCE inflation to our 2 percent objective." Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, who voted to
hold, added his own hawkish framing: "There is no soft implicit target, not on this committee's
watch." Oil's climb (Brent above $88 intraday on renewed Iran-related shipping risk) compounded the
inflation worry, since higher energy costs feed directly into the inflation numbers the Fed is
already flagging as too-high. Equities rallied through all of it — the S&P 500 gained 0.7% on the
same day the bond market was pricing a materially higher chance of a near-term hike.
([CNBC/Hammack-Kashkari](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/fed-officials-who-voted-to-hike-rates-say-action-is-needed-now-against-inflation.html),
[CNBC/Warsh analysis](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/kevin-warsh-fed-inflation-rate-hike-markets.html),
[CNBC/yields](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/treasury-yields-inflation-interest-rates-federal-reserve.html))

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** this is the same FOMC meeting Thursday's brief scored as "data sided
with the doves" (core PCE in line, GDP soft) — but Friday's follow-through commentary from the
meeting's three dissenters shows the hawkish wing didn't go quiet after being outvoted 9–3, they
went to the press. **Inference (medium-high confidence):** a bond market pricing higher near-term
hike odds while equities keep rallying is not a stable combination — either equities are right that
inflation risk is overstated, or bonds are right and equities haven't caught up yet, because higher
long-term yields eventually raise the discount rate applied to future earnings, which hits
high-multiple AI/growth names hardest.

➡️ *What you could do:* Watch the 30-year yield, not the Fed's next meeting date, for the highest-
frequency read on this fight — it's already at a post-2007 high, meaning the bond market is pricing
this risk in real time rather than waiting for the next FOMC decision. If 30-year yields keep
climbing while SPY keeps making new highs, that gap is the thing to watch for a sudden repricing,
not a specific calendar date.

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; expected S&P volatility. Low (<16) = calm; high (>25) = stressed.
- **AAII survey** — weekly poll of retail investors on whether they're bullish/bearish for the next 6 months.
- **FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee)** — the Fed's rate-setting committee; votes on the benchmark federal funds rate at each meeting, and dissents (votes against the majority decision) are a signal of internal disagreement about the right policy path.
- **Dissent (Fed context)** — when a voting FOMC member votes against the majority decision; multiple dissents, especially all in the same direction (here, favoring a hike), signal the committee is more divided than the headline "held rates steady" suggests.
- **10-year / 30-year Treasury yield** — the interest rate the US government pays to borrow over those terms; rising yields raise borrowing costs economy-wide and increase the discount rate applied to future corporate earnings, which pressures high-multiple growth stocks most.
- **REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust)** — a company that owns income-producing real estate (here, data centers); REITs are typically rate-sensitive because their valuations depend on discounting long-dated rental cash flows.
- **Capex (capital expenditures)** — money a company spends building long-term assets (here, AI data centers); a rising capex guidance range signals more planned spending, not necessarily worse near-term profitability.
- **ETF proxy** — an exchange-traded fund used to represent an index or asset (SPY for the S&P, GLD for gold); ETF proxies only trade during US market hours, unlike crypto or oil futures.
- **SOX / SMH** — the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and its tracking ETF (SMH); the standard benchmark for chip-sector performance.
- **Brent vs. WTI** — the two main global oil benchmarks; Brent is the international reference price, WTI is the US domestic one. They usually move together but can diverge on regional supply/demand shocks.
- **HBM (High-Bandwidth Memory)** — a premium, AI-server-specific memory chip category; a key battleground for pricing power between Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix.
- **Basis point / bp** — one-hundredth of a percentage point; a 25bp move is 0.25%, the Fed's standard rate-adjustment increment.
