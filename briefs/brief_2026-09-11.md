## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** CNBC and multiple wire outlets led Thursday's PPI coverage with
"wholesale inflation rose 5.4%" (year-over-year) — a headline number that fed directly into the
day's Fed-hike-odds narrative (Section 4) and was widely cited as confirmation that inflation
pressure is broadening.

**What the primary document actually says:** The BLS's own August 2026 Producer Price Index news
release (bls.gov/news.release/ppi.htm; DOL release ppi_09102026.pdf) shows core PPI — excluding
food and energy — rose just **0.2% month-over-month, undershooting the 0.3% consensus forecast**,
at 4.6% year-over-year. Over three-fourths of the entire headline increase in final-demand goods
came from energy prices (+4.2%), and **diesel fuel alone, up 24.1% for the month, accounted for
more than a third of the goods increase** and nearly two-thirds of the rise in intermediate-demand
processed goods.

**The gap:** The 5.4% headline number is accurate, but the release itself attributes almost all
of it to a single, identifiable cause — the oil-driven diesel spike (Section 4, Section 7) — not
a broadening of underlying producer costs. Core PPI actually came in *below* forecast. A reader
who saw only the "wholesale inflation rose 5.4%" headline would reasonably conclude inflation
pressure is spreading; the primary release instead describes a concentrated energy pass-through
from the war, layered on an underlying core trend that's cooling, not accelerating. This matters
directly for today's CPI reaction (Section 11) and the Fed's September 16 decision: whether
policymakers weight the headline number or the core breakdown more heavily is exactly the
question this week's data is testing.

- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the
  next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25 = stressed. At 17.84 after a fourth straight daily
  increase, its highest level this week (Section 3).
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish sentiment,
  published Thursdays. Latest reading (week ending Sept 9): 38.0% bulls, 22.7% neutral, 39.3%
  bears — the first net-bearish reading this brief has tracked (Section 3).
- **PPI (Producer Price Index)** — measures prices businesses receive for their output; a
  precursor to consumer-level (CPI) inflation. August's headline 5.4% y/y was driven almost
  entirely by energy/diesel; core PPI (4.6% y/y) actually undershot forecast (Section 9.5).
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)** — measures prices consumers actually pay; the Fed's primary
  inflation gauge and today's main event (Section 11).
- **Core inflation** — CPI or PPI excluding food and energy, viewed as a cleaner read on
  underlying price trends since food/energy prices are volatile and often driven by external
  shocks like this year's oil war (Section 9.5).
- **Brent vs. WTI** — Brent is the global oil benchmark (more exposed to Middle East/Strait of
  Hormuz risk); WTI is the US domestic benchmark. Brent broke to $107-109 Thursday, a fresh high
  for this crisis (Section 4, Section 7).
- **S-1** — the SEC registration statement a company must file before an IPO, containing audited
  financials and legally binding disclosures. Anthropic confidentially filed one June 1, 2026; a
  direct EDGAR search confirmed for a sixth straight day that no public version exists yet
  (Section 10).
- **Falsifiable version** — this brief's standing discipline for speculative threads: state in
  advance what specific, checkable outcome would confirm or refute the read, then grade it
  honestly once data exists (Section 10).
