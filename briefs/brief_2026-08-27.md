## 4. The story that matters most

**Nvidia beat every number that matters and still can't fully shake off a margin story its own
CFO confirmed is real — and that story is about to test whether the market believes memory
scarcity is Nvidia's problem or Micron's opportunity.**

**Fact — the headline results.** Revenue $96.2bn (up 18% quarter-over-quarter, 106% year-over-year),
non-GAAP EPS $2.22, data-center revenue $89bn, GAAP and non-GAAP gross margin both 75.0% for the
quarter just reported. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/26/nvidia-results-q2-earnings/))

**Fact — the guidance that moved the stock.** Nvidia guided current-quarter revenue to $91bn ±2%,
and — for the first time this far in advance — guided full fiscal-2028 revenue growth to roughly
70% year-over-year, against Street estimates near 44%. Jensen Huang said "our demand is much
greater than 70%. Our supply allows us to confidently deliver 70%." Gross margin is guided to
*decline* and bottom in Q4 FY2027 at 71-72%. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/26/nvidias-70percent-sales-growth-projection-for-2028-wows-analysts-on-earnings-call.html))

**Fact — the mechanism behind the margin decline, in the CFO's own words.** In her prepared
commentary, CFO Colette Kress said: "we are experiencing extreme pricing conditions in memory.
The magnitude of the price increase has exceeded our prior expectations and are headed even
higher into next year," and — critically — "memory scarcity today is being driven in large part
by the AI build-out itself." (See Section 9.5 for the full primary-source comparison.)

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** Nvidia's own margin pressure and Micron/Western Digital's pricing
power are the same fact described from opposite sides of the same transaction — Nvidia is a
buyer of the memory whose price is spiking, and Micron/WDC are the sellers benefiting from it.
**Inference (medium confidence):** because Kress explicitly said the AI build-out itself is
driving memory scarcity, this isn't an external supply shock Nvidia is absorbing — it's a
partially self-inflicted cost of the very capex boom Nvidia is selling into, which means the
margin pressure should persist as long as the buildout continues, not resolve as a one-quarter
blip.

➡️ *What you could do:* Track NVDA's gross margin against its own 71-72% Q4 FY2027 floor guide
over the next two quarters — a margin that holds at or above 72% would say Nvidia is managing the
memory-cost pass-through better than guided (bullish surprise); a break below 71% would confirm
the memory squeeze is worse than management modeled, and would likely pressure the stock more
than any single quarter's revenue beat could offset.

## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** The dominant framing across financial media (CNBC, Fortune, Yahoo
Finance) was "Nvidia crushes estimates" paired with "stock falls despite the beat" — largely
treating the margin concern as a generic, somewhat mysterious investor overreaction to an
otherwise flawless quarter, with the story resolving into "70% guidance reassures the market."

**What the primary document actually says.** Nvidia's own CFO commentary letter (filed with the
SEC alongside the earnings release) states plainly: *"we are experiencing extreme pricing
conditions in memory. The magnitude of the price increase has exceeded our prior expectations and
are headed even higher into next year,"* and — the more pointed line — *"memory scarcity today is
being driven in large part by the AI build-out itself."*

**The gap.** Most headline coverage treated the margin/memory-cost story as a passing footnote to
the "big beat, big guide" narrative. Nvidia's own CFO, in the primary document, is describing
something structurally different and more durable: the AI-datacenter boom Nvidia itself is
selling into is the direct cause of the memory scarcity now compressing Nvidia's own margins — a
self-reinforcing cost loop, not an external, transient supply shock. That distinction matters for
how long the margin pressure should be expected to last (as long as the buildout continues, per
management's own framing) versus how a "supply shock" story would typically resolve (once a
specific bottleneck clears). It also directly explains *why* Micron and Western Digital are
rallying the same week Nvidia is guiding margins down — they're not two separate stories, they're
the same fact reported from the buyer's income statement versus the sellers'.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** this is a case where going to the primary filing changes the
duration of the read, not just the framing — "transient memory shock" and "structural,
buildout-driven scarcity" imply very different timelines for when Nvidia's margins recover.
**Inference (medium confidence):** if Kress's framing is right, expect continued strength in
Micron/WDC/SanDisk relative to Nvidia's own margin trajectory for multiple quarters, not just
through the next earnings cycle.

➡️ *What you could do:* When headlines eventually declare the "memory crunch" resolved, check
whether that's based on Nvidia's own margin recovering (a lagging, buyer-side signal) or on
memory suppliers' pricing/allocation commentary easing (the more direct, primary-source signal) —
the two won't necessarily move together on the same timeline.

## 10. Connect the Dots *(most speculative — facts flagged, inferences flagged)*

**Thread one — the market is being asked to hold two contradictory beliefs about AI capex at the
same time, and Wednesday's data didn't force a choice yet.** **Fact:** Nvidia guided to 70%
FY2028 revenue growth specifically because supply, not demand, is the binding constraint —
management's own words. **Fact:** the same earnings call revealed that a key input to that
supply chain (memory) is scarce specifically *because of* the AI buildout Nvidia's own guidance
depends on continuing. **Common-sense logic:** if memory scarcity gets materially worse as the
buildout accelerates toward that 70% growth path, Nvidia's own supply constraint (already the
stated limiting factor) tightens further, which could either force guidance down later or force
memory suppliers' pricing power higher still — the two outcomes aren't independent. **Inference
(medium confidence):** Marvell's custom-products revenue tonight (Section 5) is a partial test
of this — if hyperscaler custom-chip demand is also outstripping the same supply constraints, it
corroborates that the bottleneck (and the resulting memory pricing power) is systemic across the
AI-buildout supply chain, not specific to Nvidia. **Falsifiable version I will grade:** if Micron
or Western Digital's own next earnings call (SanDisk/WDC typically report in early October)
explicitly cites easing memory pricing pressure, that would say the scarcity Kress described
Wednesday resolved faster than her own framing implied.

**Thread two — three straight declining VIX closes through the week's two biggest catalysts is a
statement about positioning, not just sentiment.** **Fact:** VIX fell Monday-through-Wednesday
(15.85 → 15.45 → 15.21) even as Nvidia's after-hours move swung as much as 8 percentage points
(−3% to +5%) in a single evening. **Common-sense logic:** VIX prices *index-wide* 30-day expected
volatility, so a single mega-cap stock's overnight chop, however dramatic, only shows up in VIX to
the extent options traders expect it to spread into broader index-level moves. **Inference (medium
confidence):** the fact that it didn't move VIX suggests options positioning currently treats
Nvidia's earnings volatility as idiosyncratic (contained to NVDA and its direct suppliers) rather
than systemic (a read-through to the S&P 500 broadly) — which is a specific, checkable stance that
today's regular session can confirm or break. **Falsifiable version I will grade:** if today's
session pushes VIX back above 16 on Nvidia-related trading, that would say the market changed its
mind and started pricing Nvidia's margin story as a systemic AI-capex risk, not an idiosyncratic
one.

numbers were a clean beat across the board — revenue, EPS, and data-center revenue all above
Street estimates — but the stock's initial 3% after-hours drop, before recovering roughly 5% off
that low on an unusually specific 70% FY2028 growth guide, shows the market genuinely wrestling
with two competing facts from the same earnings call: durable, supply-constrained demand versus a
margin squeeze CFO Colette Kress explicitly tied to memory scarcity "driven in large part by the
AI build-out itself." That framing — confirmed against Nvidia's own primary-source commentary,
not just secondhand headlines (Section 9.5) — explains why Micron, Western Digital, and the
optics names kept rising the same morning Nvidia itself was down 1.69%: they're on opposite sides
of the same cost transaction, and the market is now pricing that distinction explicitly rather
than treating "AI-infra" as one undifferentiated basket, a genuine change from how Monday's and
Tuesday's sessions traded.

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25 = stressed. Closed 15.21 Wednesday, a third straight lower close.
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish sentiment, published Thursdays. Historical averages: 37.5% bulls, 31.0% neutral, 31.5% bears.
- **Core PCE** — the Personal Consumption Expenditures price index excluding food and energy, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge. July's reading printed 3.3% YoY, exactly at consensus, the last one before the Fed's pre-FOMC blackout period.
- **Gross margin** — the percentage of revenue left after direct production costs; Nvidia's is compressing because a key input (memory chips) is getting more expensive, per CFO commentary (Section 9.5).
- **CFO commentary letter** — a supplementary document companies file alongside earnings (often as an SEC exhibit) with more candid, prepared remarks than the press release; the primary source used in Section 9.5.
- **Eligible custom-products revenue** — the specific revenue metric governing the pace of Google's warrant-based dilution arrangement with Marvell; the figure this brief is watching in tonight's report.
- **Behind-the-meter (BTM) / AI-infra basket** — the power (Constellation, Vistra, Talen, GE Vernova), cooling (Vertiv), optics (Coherent, Lumentum/Lite), and data-center (Equinix, Digital Realty) names that have historically traded together as a single sentiment basket on AI capex news — a pattern this brief's Scorecard (Section 1) now sees breaking down.
- **SMH** — the main semiconductor ETF and standard benchmark for chip-sector performance. Closed 555.68 Wednesday, flat, masking a real divergence among its underlying holdings (Section 8).
- **Falsifiable version** — this brief's standing discipline for speculative threads: state in advance what specific, checkable outcome would confirm or refute the read, so the next day's Scorecard has something objective to grade.
- **Jackson Hole** — the Kansas City Fed's annual economic policy symposium, August 27-29, 2026. Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first keynote there, Friday, August 28, 8:00am ET.
