## 9.5 What the headlines missed

**What the headlines said:** Multiple outlets this month (Bloomberg, Fortune, and others) have
reported that Anthropic is pitching a **$2 trillion IPO valuation**, targeting the **largest IPO in
history**, on the strength of a claimed **$30 trillion total addressable market** — coverage that
consistently attributes these figures to "people familiar with the matter" or investor pitch
materials.

**What the primary document actually says:** Anthropic's own official statement on its S-1
filing — published June 1, 2026 under SEC Rule 135, the only document the company itself has
issued on this topic — is three short paragraphs. It confirms the company confidentially submitted
a draft registration statement, notes that "the number of shares to be offered and the price have
not yet been set," and states explicitly that any IPO "will depend on market conditions and other
factors." It contains **no valuation figure, no TAM figure, no revenue figure, and no timeline**
beyond the fact of the filing itself.

**The gap:** every specific number driving this month's Anthropic-IPO coverage — the $2 trillion
valuation, the $30 trillion TAM, the $100–120 billion 2026 revenue projection — comes from
reporting citing unnamed sources or pitch-deck materials, not from anything Anthropic has put its
own name to. That's not evidence the numbers are wrong; a $65 billion-plus current revenue run-rate
is independently corroborated across multiple outlets. But a reader skimming headlines could easily
come away thinking Anthropic itself disclosed a $2 trillion target, when the company's only public
statement on the matter is a boilerplate compliance notice that discloses nothing about size.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** there is a real, checkable distance between what Anthropic has
published and what is being reported about Anthropic. **Inference (medium confidence):** this is a
structural feature of the confidential-S-1 process, not unique spin by any one outlet — the SEC's
Rule 135 framework is specifically designed to let a company announce *that* it filed without
disclosing *what* it filed, which creates a vacuum that pitch-deck leaks and source-based reporting
naturally fill. The gap will close on its own once a public S-1 exists.

➡️ *What you could do:* Treat every Anthropic valuation/TAM number you read this month as
sourced-not-disclosed until the public S-1 lands — and specifically note the difference between
"Anthropic filed for an IPO" (fact, confirmed by Anthropic itself) and "Anthropic is seeking a $2
trillion valuation" (reported, not yet confirmed by any document with legal disclosure
obligations).

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the next
  30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25 = stressed. 14.43 this morning, unchanged since Friday
  (Section 3).
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish sentiment,
  published Thursdays. Latest (week ending Aug 26): 32.9% bulls, 22.6% neutral, 44.4% bears; next
  reading Thursday, September 3 (Section 3, Section 10).
- **CME FedWatch** — a tool that converts fed funds futures prices into an implied probability of
  the Fed's next rate move; currently ~57% for a September hike, up steadily since the Warsh
  speech (Section 4).
- **Kalshi** — a regulated U.S. prediction-market exchange where traders bet directly on binary
  event outcomes; currently pricing the same September-hike question at ~48%, a widening
  divergence from CME FedWatch (Section 4).
- **Rule 135** — an SEC provision letting a company publicly confirm it has confidentially filed
  for an IPO without disclosing financial details; the basis of Anthropic's boilerplate June 1
  announcement (Section 9.5).
- **S-1** — the SEC registration statement a company must file before an IPO, containing audited
  financials and legally binding disclosures. Anthropic filed one confidentially in June; no
  public version exists yet (Section 5, Section 9.5).
- **TAM (total addressable market)** — the total revenue opportunity a company claims it could
  theoretically capture; a company-supplied or press-reported estimate, not an audited figure.
  Anthropic's reported $30 trillion TAM traces to press sourcing, not any Anthropic document
  (Section 9.5).
- **Revenue-recognition timing** — when a company can count revenue as "earned" under accounting
  rules, distinct from when a deal is signed or its total size is known; the core of the Marvell/
  Broadcom test (Section 5, Section 10 Thread One).
- **SMH** — the main semiconductor ETF and standard benchmark for chip-sector performance. Closed
  −3.44% Friday, unchanged since (Section 9).
- **Falsifiable version** — this brief's standing discipline for speculative threads: state in
  advance what specific, checkable outcome would confirm or refute the read, so the next day's
  Scorecard has something objective to grade.
