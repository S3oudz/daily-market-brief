strikes framed each event as a discrete incident — "smoke seen near Aramco facility," "tanker hit
in Strait of Hormuz" — reported day by day, attack by attack (Reuters, Investing.com, oilprice.com,
via search).

**What the primary data actually says:** the IEA's own estimate, reported in August (via
Bloomberg, via search), is that more than 10 million barrels per day of Gulf oil production has
been shut in, and Gulf oil exports are running at roughly half their pre-war level. Separately,
the G7 is reportedly considering releasing up to 100 million barrels of emergency oil and diesel
reserves to offset fuel costs (oilprice.com, via search) — the kind of coordinated response
normally reserved for acute supply crises, not routine market management.

**The gap:** a reader following only the day-by-day tanker-strike headlines would not learn that
global oil supply has already absorbed a shock of historic scale, or that the G7 is responding
with emergency-reserve measures that imply officials see this as more serious than any single
day's attack report suggests. **Label: FACT** on the IEA's shut-in/export estimate and the G7's
reserve-release consideration (both via search, citing Bloomberg/oilprice.com reporting rather
than a primary document this brief fetched directly); **PREDICTION/my read** that this aggregate
picture — not any individual tanker strike — is the more important fact for where oil prices go
next, and it's the one getting the least daily coverage.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** the scale of Gulf supply disruption already underway is larger than
the piecemeal daily headlines convey. **Inference (medium confidence):** that's also the most
likely explanation for why Friday's WTI print ($91.11) isn't dramatically higher despite months of
this conflict — a G7 reserve release, even just under consideration, functions as a price cap
traders can lean on, which is a very different dynamic than an uncapped supply shock.

➡️ *What you could do:* When reading future Hormuz/Aramco headlines, weight them against this
aggregate IEA/G7 context rather than treating each one as a fresh, independent data point — the
real question is whether the G7's reserve-release option stays merely "under consideration" or

### Glossary
- **Basis point (bp)** — 1/100th of a percentage point.
- **FOMC** — the Federal Open Market Committee, the Fed body that sets interest-rate policy.
  September meeting minutes due Wednesday, October 7 (Sections 10, 11).
- **CME FedWatch** — a tool that converts fed-funds futures prices into implied probabilities of
  a rate move at the next FOMC meeting; last showed October-hike odds near 17–18% after Friday's
  jobs report (prior brief, Section 4).
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the
  next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25–30 = genuine stress. Currently 15.31, set before this
  weekend's Iran-war news (Section 3).
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish sentiment;
  the bull-bear spread (bulls minus bears) is the standard summary metric. Unchanged at −11.9;
  next reading Thursday, October 8 (Section 1).
- **WTI vs. Brent crude** — two different crude oil benchmarks; WTI is the US domestic benchmark,
  Brent is the international (North Sea) benchmark and is more directly exposed to Middle East
  supply disruptions (Section 7).
- **UKMTO** — the UK Maritime Trade Operations body, which monitors and reports on shipping
  incidents (including attacks) in the Gulf and Strait of Hormuz (Section 4).
- **IEA** — the International Energy Agency, an intergovernmental body that tracks and estimates
  global oil supply and demand, including shut-in production during supply disruptions (Sections
  4, 9.5).
- **8-K** — a filing public companies must make with the SEC within days of a material corporate
  event (an executive appointment, a merger closing, etc.); the primary-source document for
  Section 5's Skydance/Kreiz facts.
- **RSU (restricted stock unit)** — a form of equity compensation that converts to actual shares
  once vesting conditions are met; part of Kreiz's pay package (Section 5).
