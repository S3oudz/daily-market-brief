PMI, stabilizing yields) and priced them fully — VIX fell 5.50%, all four index proxies rose,

## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said.** Since Thursday, the dominant framing has been that of an active
blockade: President Trump declared an **"ECONOMIC D-DAY"** against Iran, stated the **US naval
blockade of Iranian ports remains in effect and that mines in the Strait of Hormuz are in place**,
and Bessent has promised **"the toughest sanctions in history"** to formalize that pressure Monday.
Coverage through the weekend has largely repeated this blockade framing without revisiting it
against the physical shipping data.

**What the primary shipping-tracking data actually says.** Maritime intelligence firm **Kpler**,
whose data has been cited by CNN Business in its own reporting on August 17–19, found that **more
than 80% of liquids transits through the Strait of Hormuz over the past two weeks took the
UN-authorized Omani route** — a corridor Iran opposes and cannot toll — **or were "dark" transits
(transponders off) that likely took the same route.** A month earlier, Kpler observed **virtually
no shipping traffic via the Omani route**, meaning this is a rapid, recent shift. **Kuwait, Saudi
Arabia and the UAE have chartered Very Large Crude Carriers** to exit the Gulf via Hormuz and then
transfer cargo to customers' tankers outside what shippers consider the "danger zone." Flow
estimates vary by source: one account put the seven-day average of oil exiting the Gulf at **9
million barrels a day**, while ship-tracking data suggested roughly **half that figure**. Either
reading is a meaningful recovery from the **EIA's Q2 2026 average of 4.9 million barrels a day**,
though both remain well below the roughly **15 million barrels a day** that transited in 2025
before the conflict — a shortfall of somewhere between **40% and 70%**, depending on which flow
estimate is used.
([CNN Business](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/18/business/iran-strait-of-hormuz-oil),
[CNN Business, Aug 17](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/17/business/oil-market-strait-of-hormuz-trump))

**The gap:** the "blockade in effect, mines in place" framing implies oil flow through Hormuz is
being actively stopped. The shipping-tracking data — some of it published by the same outlets
carrying the blockade framing — describes something closer to an **adaptive reroute around
Iranian-controlled waters**: shippers going dark, using an alternate UN-sanctioned corridor, and
transferring cargo outside the conflict zone, with volumes recovering (on the higher estimate,
nearly doubling from the Q2 average) even as Iran's ability to interdict or toll that traffic
has visibly eroded. Neither the "blockade is total" framing nor a "the crisis is over" framing is
accurate; the data supports a narrower, checkable claim: **the physical chokepoint has partially
reopened via workaround, faster than the political rhetoric has updated to reflect.**

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** oil flow through Hormuz has recovered meaningfully from its Q2 low
even as the rhetorical framing around the strait ("blockade," "mines in place") has not softened.
**Inference (medium-high confidence):** this matters directly for Monday's sanctions
announcement — if physical oil flow is already adapting around the chokepoint via a route
Washington and its Gulf allies control (the Omani corridor, backed by the US Navy per prior
reporting), then the *marginal* lever left to genuinely tighten oil supply is exactly the one
Bessent is about to pull: **secondary sanctions on the buyers and financiers of Iranian crude**,
not further pressure on the physical strait itself. **Inference (medium confidence):** this is a
reason to weight the "secondary sanctions" scenario somewhat more heavily than the market's flat
Friday oil print suggests it currently is — the physical-blockade lever has been substantially
worked around, so a serious policy response to that reality plausibly shifts toward financial
enforcement, which is precisely the domain Bessent's Monday announcement covers.

➡️ *What you could do:* Before reading Monday's coverage of the sanctions announcement, check
whether it addresses the **Omani-route workaround** directly — Treasury's own designation list, if
it targets **shipping registries or the entities facilitating the "dark" transits and ship-to-ship
transfers**, would be a direct, verifiable response to exactly the loophole Kpler's data describes.
If the announcement is silent on shipping/registry entities and focuses only on financial
institutions, that is itself informative about how seriously Washington is treating the physical
workaround versus the financial flows. Either way, don't take "blockade" language in Monday's
coverage at face value without checking it against the transit data — the gap between the two has
been growing for a week.

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25 = stressed. At 15.13 it implies roughly a 0.95% average daily S&P move.
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on whether they are bullish or bearish about the next six months, published Thursdays. Historical averages: 37.5% bulls, 31.0% neutral, 31.5% bears.
- **Flash PMI (Purchasing Managers' Index)** — a preliminary monthly survey of business activity; above 50 signals expansion. S&P Global's August flash composite reading of 56.0 was the highest since April 2022.
- **Primary vs secondary sanctions** — primary sanctions bar a country's own citizens and firms from dealing with a target; secondary sanctions punish *third-country* firms and financiers for doing so. Secondary sanctions are far more consequential for oil markets because they target rerouted flows rather than the blockaded country directly.
- **Warrant / contingent vesting** — a contract giving the holder the right to buy newly issued shares at a set price, diluting existing holders when exercised. Google's Marvell warrant (58.97m shares at $206.58) vests as eligible custom-products revenue accrues — dilution that scales with the deal's own commercial success.
- **Spot bitcoin ETF** — an exchange-traded fund holding bitcoin directly, allowing investors to gain exposure without self-custody. Concentrated single-issuer inflows (e.g., BlackRock's 83% share of a $606m day) point to programmatic institutional allocation rather than broad retail buying.
- **CLARITY Act** — proposed US legislation defining whether a given crypto asset is regulated as a security or a commodity. Its procedural Senate vote is now scheduled for Tuesday, September 15.
- **Take-or-pay contract** — a supply agreement where the buyer pays whether or not it takes delivery. Micron's 16 such agreements are the strongest available evidence against a memory boom-bust cycle.
- **Treasury buyback** — the Treasury purchasing its own outstanding bonds in the secondary market before maturity. The first scaled operation under the enlarged program begins September 9.
- **Bid-to-cover** — the ratio of offers received to the amount accepted in an auction or buyback; measures the cost of an intervention, not whether it works.
- **Announcement effect** — the price move caused by a policy being stated, before it is implemented. Purely informational, so it has no floor: newer information reprices it.
- **Real yield** — a bond's yield minus expected inflation; the opportunity cost of holding assets that pay nothing, which is why gold tends to rise when real yields fall.
- **Debasement trade** — buying assets with no issuer (gold, silver, bitcoin) on the view that government money's purchasing power will erode. Both gold and silver have now cleared their prior test levels ($4,500 / $65) comfortably.
- **Duration (equity)** — how far in the future a company's expected profits sit; longer duration means more sensitivity to interest rates. Long-duration growth names (ARKK-style, BTM power) are the ones most exposed if strong growth data keeps the Fed from cutting.
- **Behind-the-meter (BTM)** — power generation connected directly to a customer such as a data centre rather than through the public grid. The basket (Talen, Vistra, Constellation, GE Vernova, Vertiv) has traded inconsistently as either an AI-infrastructure trade or a rates trade over the past week.
- **SMH** — the main semiconductor ETF and standard benchmark for chip-sector performance.
- **ARKK** — an actively managed fund of long-duration, speculative growth equities; a proxy for risk appetite and rate sensitivity simultaneously.
- **Mechanism call vs direction call** — a call that tells you *which number decides the outcome* versus one that tells you *which way the price goes*. Mechanism calls survive being wrong about direction.
- **Strait of Hormuz / Omani route** — the chokepoint carrying a large share of seaborne oil, and the UN-authorized alternate corridor shippers are increasingly using to bypass Iranian-controlled waters. Over 80% of recent transits have used this route or gone dark to obscure their path.
- **Commercial crude inventories** — privately held US crude stocks, reported weekly by the EIA, distinct from the SPR.
- **Brent vs. WTI** — the two main oil benchmarks. Brent is the international reference price; WTI the US domestic one.
- **PCE / core PCE** — the Personal Consumption Expenditures price index, the Fed's preferred inflation measure. The July reading, released August 26, is the last before the September 15–16 FOMC meeting.
- **Jackson Hole** — the Kansas City Fed's annual economic policy symposium, August 27–29 in Wyoming. Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first keynote there at 10am ET Friday, August 28.
