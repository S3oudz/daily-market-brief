## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** Coverage of Salesforce's earnings led overwhelmingly with the beat
itself — "Salesforce crushes Q2 earnings," adjusted EPS of $5.90 against a $3.27 consensus, stock
up as much as 21.6%, framed as confirmation of "AI growth" and the new Anthropic partnership
(CNBC, TradingView, Benzinga all led with the beat-and-guidance-raise angle).

**What the primary numbers actually say.** Salesforce's own reported breakdown shows that **$2.53
of the $2.63 beat versus consensus came from a mark-to-market gain on its strategic investment in
Anthropic** — not from the core subscription and services business. Strip out the investment
gain and adjusted EPS comes to roughly $3.37, against $2.91 a year ago — about 16% growth, not
the ~80% headline beat the raw EPS number implies. Operating income was reported roughly flat
year-on-year. ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/27/salesforce-stock-just-soared-but-investment-gains-delivered-usd2-53-of-its-usd5-90-in-per-share-profit/), [EBC Financial Group](https://www.ebc.com/forex/salesforce-stock-q2-fy27-eps-investment-gains))

**The gap.** Most coverage treated the $5.90 adjusted-EPS figure as the headline number and the
Anthropic stake as a nice side story about the AI partnership, rather than leading with the fact
that the stake's mark-to-market gain is what turned an in-line-ish core quarter into a headline
"crushes estimates" story. That distinction matters for anyone using Thursday's 21.6% stock pop
as evidence of accelerating core-business demand: the raised full-year guidance ($16.67-$16.71
adjusted EPS, up from $14.06-$14.12) reflects both real operating momentum *and* a reduced share
count from the investment gains — the two are blended in the guidance the way they were blended
in the quarter, and coverage generally didn't separate them.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** this is a case where the primary numbers don't contradict the
headline (Salesforce did beat and did raise guidance) but they change the *composition* of the
story — a company beating because its core business accelerated is a different signal than one
beating because an equity stake it holds went up in value. **Inference (medium confidence):** if
Salesforce's core, ex-investment-gain growth rate (~16%) is the more representative number for
judging the business, that's a solid-but-not-extraordinary quarter — closer to Marvell's "good
but not extraordinary enough" pattern (Section 4) than to Nvidia's genuinely surprising 70% guide,
even though Thursday's stock reaction (+21.6%) looked more like Nvidia's.

➡️ *What you could do:* When a company's EPS beat is unusually large relative to revenue growth,
check the earnings release for one-time or investment-related items before treating the stock's
reaction as pure confirmation of core-business acceleration — Salesforce's $2.53-of-$2.63 gap is
exactly the kind of number that's disclosed in the primary filing but easy to miss in headline
coverage.

## 10. Connect the Dots *(most speculative — facts flagged, inferences flagged)*

**Thread one — the market is discriminating on the *size* of the demand surprise before it'll
look past margin-pressure guidance, and Broadcom's report next week is the next test.**
**Fact:** Nvidia guided memory-driven margin pressure Wednesday and guided 70% FY28 revenue
growth (vs. 44% Street) — the stock rallied 8.77% Thursday. **Fact:** Marvell guided
custom-silicon-driven margin pressure Thursday night, with revenue beating by a comparatively
modest amount relative to a stock that had already run 187% YTD — the stock fell 7.6%
after-hours. **Common-sense logic:** if the pattern is "big enough demand surprise offsets margin
caution, in-line demand surprise doesn't," then the market's bar isn't about margin guidance at
all — it's about whether the growth number changes the multi-year revenue story enough to matter.
**Inference (medium confidence):** Broadcom reports Tuesday, September 2, after guiding its own
AI-related growth expectations for months; if Broadcom also flags margin pressure (memory or
custom-silicon related) alongside its guide, whether the stock rallies or falls will be a real
test of this threshold theory. **Falsifiable version I will grade:** if Broadcom guides margin
pressure alongside revenue growth in the 35-45% range (closer to Marvell's magnitude than
Nvidia's), and the stock still rallies, that would say the "size of surprise" theory is wrong and
something else (sector-wide momentum, positioning) is driving the split instead.

**Thread two — AAII's bear reading jumped the same week the market posted its best rally in
weeks, and that gap is either a lagging indicator or an early warning.** **Fact:** AAII bears rose
from 39.9% to 44.4% (bulls fell from 35.5% to 32.9%) in the week-ending-Aug-26 survey, while the
Nasdaq Composite rose 1.57% and VIX fell to 14.51 over the same window. **Common-sense logic:**
AAII surveys retail sentiment with a lag (responses typically collected mid-week, before
Thursday's rally fully registered), so part of the gap is timing — but a 4.5-point bear increase
during a week that included Wednesday's PCE-in-line print is still a real divergence from how
professional positioning (VIX, the rally itself) read the same week. **Inference (medium
confidence):** two explanations are live: (a) retail sentiment is simply lagging and will catch
up in next week's reading once Thursday's rally is reflected, which would make this week's
reading a stale, low-value data point; or (b) retail investors are pricing something specific —
plausibly Warsh's keynote today, or valuation concerns after a 187%-type run in names like
Marvell — that hasn't shown up in options pricing yet. **Falsifiable version I will grade:** if
next week's AAII reading (due Thursday, September 3) reverses back toward bulls, that supports
explanation (a) — a lag, not a signal; if bears keep climbing even as the market holds its gains,
that supports (b) — retail was early on something the options market hasn't priced yet.

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25 = stressed. Closed 14.51 this morning, a fourth straight lower reading.
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish sentiment, published Thursdays. Historical averages: 37.5% bulls, 31.0% neutral, 31.5% bears. This week: 32.9% bulls, 22.6% neutral, 44.4% bears.
- **Mark-to-market gain** — an accounting adjustment that revalues an asset (like Salesforce's Anthropic equity stake) to its current market value, booked as income even though no cash changed hands; the source of most of Salesforce's Thursday EPS beat (Section 9.5).
- **Custom-products / custom-silicon revenue** — chips designed to a specific customer's specifications (e.g., Marvell's work for Google's TPU ecosystem), as opposed to off-the-shelf chips; the metric this brief has tracked as a read on hyperscaler AI-capex demand.
- **Warrant** — a security giving the holder the right to buy shares at a set price; Google's Marvell warrant vests in tranches tied to cumulative chip purchases, up to $120bn over 6.5 years (Section 5).
- **Gross margin** — the percentage of revenue left after direct production costs; both Nvidia (memory costs) and Marvell (custom-silicon ramp costs) guided margin pressure this week, with opposite stock reactions (Section 4).
- **SMH** — the main semiconductor ETF and standard benchmark for chip-sector performance. Closed +3.09% Thursday, a clean rally signal, with Marvell as the notable exception (Section 9).
- **Falsifiable version** — this brief's standing discipline for speculative threads: state in advance what specific, checkable outcome would confirm or refute the read, so the next day's Scorecard has something objective to grade.
- **Jackson Hole** — the Kansas City Fed's annual economic policy symposium, August 27-29, 2026. Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first keynote there today, Friday, August 28, 10:00am ET.
