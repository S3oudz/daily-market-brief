### Item one — two mega-cap retailers, the same $1bn-scale tariff windfall, opposite share-price outcomes, and the coverage led with the windfall both times

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25 = stressed. At 16.01 it implies roughly a 1.01% average daily S&P move.
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on whether they are bullish or bearish about the next six months, published Thursdays. Historical averages: 37.5% bulls, 31.0% neutral, 31.5% bears. Readings near 50%+ bears have historically marked contrarian bottoms; 39.9% is elevated, not extreme.
- **Neutral camp (AAII)** — the share of respondents who are undecided. Worth watching separately from the bull-bear spread, because undecided investors are disproportionately the ones who buy pullbacks. It fell to 24.6% this week from 27.4%.
- **Bull-bear spread** — bulls minus bears, in percentage points. Negative means more pessimists than optimists. This week: −4.4, wider than last week's −3.2.
- **Comparable sales ("comps")** — sales growth from stores and channels open at least a year, excluding new-store contribution. The cleanest measure of whether a retailer is actually selling more; it cannot be inflated by a tax or tariff refund. Walmart's +2.6% missed a 3.7–3.8% expectation; Target's +3.8% beat a 2.4% expectation.
- **Transactions vs ticket** — the two components of a comp. Transactions = number of visits; ticket = average spend per visit. Transactions outgrowing ticket (Walmart: +1.5% vs +1.1%) is the signature of a budget-constrained customer who is still shopping but buying less per trip.
- **IEEPA (International Emergency Economic Powers Act)** — the emergency statute used to impose certain tariffs. The US Supreme Court ruled in **February 2026** that it did not grant unilateral tariff authority, which is why importers are receiving refunds of duties already paid. Walmart received nearly $2.9bn; Target $994m pretax.
- **Tariff refund (as an earnings item)** — a recovery of previously paid import duties. Genuine cash, but non-recurring: it inflates one quarter's profit and makes the following year's comparison worse by the same amount. Watch for it disclosed as a share of *operating income growth* (Walmart: 750bp of ~17.4%), not just as EPS.
- **Basis point (bp)** — one hundredth of a percentage point. 750bp = 7.5 percentage points; 10bp on a bond yield = 0.10%.
- **Constant currency** — a growth rate with exchange-rate movements stripped out, so it reflects the underlying business rather than the dollar. Walmart guides in constant currency.
- **Take-or-pay contract** — a supply agreement where the buyer pays whether or not it takes delivery. In memory chips this is the strongest available evidence against a boom-bust cycle, because it converts a forecast into a contractual obligation. Micron reported 16 such strategic customer agreements.
- **Treasury buyback** — the Treasury purchasing its own outstanding bonds in the secondary market before maturity. A debt-management and liquidity tool; when concentrated in the long end during a yield spike, also a way to support prices. The August 19 announcement doubled the per-operation size cap for 10-to-30-year maturities to a minimum of $4bn, effective September 9.
- **Bid-to-cover** — the ratio of offers received to the amount accepted in an auction or buyback. In a buyback, heavy offers mean many holders wanted out; light offers mean the intervention was cheap. It measures the *cost* of an intervention, not whether it works.
- **Announcement effect** — the price move caused by a policy being stated, before it is implemented. It is purely informational and therefore has no floor: newer information reprices it. Wednesday's 10bp rally was roughly half to three-quarters reversed in one session.
- **Real yield** — a bond's yield minus expected inflation. It is the opportunity cost of holding an asset that pays nothing, which is why gold rises when real yields fall — and why an oil shock, which raises expected inflation, pushes nominal yields up while supporting gold.
- **Primary vs secondary sanctions** — primary sanctions bar your own citizens and firms from dealing with a target; secondary sanctions punish *third-country* firms for doing so. Secondary sanctions are far more consequential for oil prices because they attack the rerouted flows (Chinese refiners, ship registries, swap lines) rather than the blockaded country.
- **Supply shock** — an inflation impulse from the cost side (oil, shipping, tariffs) rather than the demand side. Central banks find it hardest to address because raising rates does not produce more oil. July's three FOMC dissenters named supply shocks as their central worry.
- **Debasement trade** — buying assets with no issuer (gold, silver, bitcoin) on the view that the purchasing power of government money will be eroded by deficits, inflation, or policy that suppresses returns on government debt. Distinguishable from a safe-haven trade because it needs no crisis, only a policy direction. The **hard test** is whether it holds on a day yields rise and the dollar firms — which it did Thursday.
- **Fiscal put / monetary put** — market shorthand for a belief that the fiscal or monetary authority will intervene to stop asset prices falling. A "put" in this sense is an expectation, not a contract, which is why it can be revoked — or simply overtaken by an oil price.
- **CLARITY Act** — proposed US legislation defining whether a given crypto asset is regulated as a security or a commodity. Stalled in the Senate with a procedural vote scheduled for September. Structurally more significant than an SEC rule because legislation is not revocable by the next administration.
- **Regulation Crypto Assets (SEC, S7-2026-27)** — the SEC's proposed permanent framework for digital assets, with registration exemptions at $5m/four years and $75m/12 months, a safe harbour, and preemption of state registration. A **proposal**, not a rule; 60-day comment period.
- **Short liquidation / short squeeze** — forced buying when traders betting on a decline are closed out by rising prices. It amplifies a move mechanically and is self-limiting, because the fuel runs out. 91% of Wednesday's $1.91bn of crypto liquidations were shorts, which is why the *following* days matter more than the day itself.
- **Warrant / contingent vesting** — a contract giving the holder the right to buy newly issued shares at a set price, diluting existing holders when exercised. Google's Marvell warrant (58.97m shares at $206.58) vests one tranche per $500m of eligible custom-products revenue — dilution priced in delivered business rather than in cash.
- **ETF proxy** — an ETF used to stand in for an index or commodity (SPY for the S&P 500, GLD for gold, USO for oil). USO holds front-month futures and loses value to roll costs, so it tracks daily oil moves but is a poor long-term holding. Where an ETF proxy's magnitude disagrees with the cash market (as SLV did Thursday), trust the cash figure.
- **SMH** — the main semiconductor ETF and the standard benchmark for chip-sector performance.
- **ARKK** — an actively managed fund of long-duration, speculative growth equities. A proxy for risk appetite and rate sensitivity simultaneously, which is why separating the two requires comparing it against something else.
- **Duration (equity)** — how far in the future a company's expected profits sit. Longer duration means more sensitivity to interest rates, because distant profits are discounted more heavily. It is why the BTM power names now trade like bonds.
- **Behind-the-meter (BTM)** — power generation connected directly to a customer such as a data centre rather than through the public grid. The BTM basket (Talen, Vistra, Constellation, GE Vernova, Vertiv) was conceived as an AI-infrastructure trade; on two consecutive sessions it has behaved as rate duration instead.
- **Positioning move vs information move** — a price change caused by who owns the asset versus one caused by new facts. A large decline with no sourceable catalyst is the signature of the former, and it ends when sellers are done rather than when news changes. Thursday's component bounce, explained only as "oversold dip-buying," is the same category in reverse.
- **Mechanism call vs direction call** — a call that tells you *which number decides the outcome* versus one that tells you *which way the price goes*. Mechanism calls survive being wrong about direction; this brief's two best calls this week were both mechanism calls.
- **Two-variable conditional (a specification error)** — a test of the form "X happens while Y moves in direction Z." It fails silently whenever X and Y share a common driver. Three of this brief's tests failed this way in three days; every level since Wednesday is single-variable.
- **DRAM / NAND / HBM** — the three main memory chip types. DRAM is working memory; NAND is storage; **HBM (High Bandwidth Memory)** is stacked, very fast memory beside an AI accelerator and the current binding constraint on AI server production.
- **DDR5 RDIMM** — the enterprise/server form of DRAM; its contract price is the standard benchmark for what data-centre memory costs.
- **800G / 1.6T transceivers** — optical modules moving 800 gigabits or 1.6 terabits per second, the interconnect inside AI data centres. Record 800G shipments and rising 1.6T adoption are what drove Lumentum's +109% year-over-year revenue quarter.
- **Lockup / share unlock** — a post-IPO period during which insiders cannot sell. When a tranche expires, tradable supply rises — a mechanical headwind independent of company news. SPCX's next 319-million-share tranche unlocks September 9, followed by an affiliate block September 10 and 328.4m shares on each of September 24, October 9 and October 24.
- **Strait of Hormuz** — the chokepoint carrying a large share of seaborne oil. EIA estimates flows fell to **4.9 million barrels a day in Q2 2026** from **21.6 million** before the conflict — a 77% collapse that has so far coincided with US commercial crude *builds*, not draws, which is why the physical-disruption story has been largely absorbed and the sanctions story has not.
- **Commercial crude inventories** — privately held US crude stocks, reported weekly by the EIA, distinct from the SPR. A **build** means supply exceeded demand that week. Two consecutive builds (+17.4m, then +4.4m) is why oil did not spike on the physical disruption alone.
- **Brent vs. WTI** — the two main oil benchmarks. Brent is the international reference price; WTI the US domestic one. Brent settled $93.78 and WTI $86.64 on Thursday.
- **PCE / core PCE** — the Personal Consumption Expenditures price index, the Fed's preferred inflation measure; "core" excludes food and energy. The July reading is the last before the September 18 Fed decision.
