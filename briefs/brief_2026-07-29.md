## 9.5 What the Headlines Missed

**What the headlines said:** SK Hynix's own release and most wire coverage (PR Newswire,
Seeking Alpha, StockTitan) on Tuesday framed the Q2 report in unambiguously positive terms —
"record" revenue and operating profit, cumulative first-half revenue crossing 100 trillion won
for the first time in company history, with revenue and operating profit up 257% and 557%
year-over-year respectively.

**What the primary data actually shows:** All of that is true and verified in the company's own
release. But the same report also missed Wall Street's own consensus estimates — expectations
had run up even faster than the 257%-YoY growth rate itself — and SK Hynix's Korea-listed shares
fell 14.7% the same day (their worst day in the current stretch), with the U.S.-listed ADR
closing at a record low. TechTimes was one of the few outlets to headline this explicitly
("Record Earnings, Record Low"); most other coverage reported the record-profit figures without
foregrounding the miss-against-consensus or the stock's actual reaction.

**The gap:** "record profit" (true, and the dominant framing) and "worst stock reaction in
months" (also true, and the market's actual verdict) both happened on the same release — they
aren't in conflict, but coverage led overwhelmingly with the former. What's underreported is that
the *gap between actual results and elevated Street expectations* — not the absolute growth
number — is what moved the stock, arriving the same week as CXMT's $488B competitive debut
(Section 5). That's a materially different, and more useful, read than "record earnings" alone
suggests.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** this is a framing-emphasis gap, not a factual error — nearly every
outlet reported the record-profit numbers accurately. **Inference (medium confidence):** the
practical lesson generalizes beyond SK Hynix — in a sector this hyped, "beat the prior quarter"
and "beat what the Street had already priced in" are increasingly different tests, and headline
coverage defaults to the easier, more positive-sounding one.

➡️ *What you could do:* When a "record earnings" headline is followed by a same-day stock drop,
check the consensus-estimate gap before assuming the headline is wrong or the stock move is
irrational — both can be true at once, and it's usually the more informative story.

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; expected S&P volatility. Low (<16) = calm; high (>25) = stressed.
- **AAII survey** — weekly poll of retail investors on whether they're bullish/bearish for the next 6 months.
- **PCE deflator (Personal Consumption Expenditures)** — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, distinct from CPI; tracked closely around FOMC meetings.
- **FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee)** — the Fed committee that sets interest rate policy; meets roughly every six weeks.
- **DRAM (Dynamic Random-Access Memory)** — general-purpose computer memory; distinct from the specialized HBM chips used in AI accelerators, though made by the same companies (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, and now CXMT).
- **HBM (High-Bandwidth Memory)** — the specialized, high-margin memory chips used in AI accelerators.
- **ADR (American Depositary Receipt)** — a U.S.-traded certificate representing shares of a foreign company (here, SK Hynix), letting U.S. investors trade it without a Korean brokerage account.
- **Kospi** — South Korea's main stock index; Samsung and SK Hynix together make up nearly half its weight.
- **ETF proxy** — an exchange-traded fund used to represent an index or asset (SPY for the S&P, GLD for gold); ETF proxies only trade during US market hours, unlike crypto or oil futures.
- **SOX / SMH** — the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and its tracking ETF (SMH); the standard benchmark for chip-sector performance.
- **Circular financing** — when a company (here, Nvidia) helps fund its own customer's (OpenAI's) purchases of its products, raising questions about how much of reported demand is organically funded versus vendor-backed.
