## 4. The story that matters most

**Over the weekend, the Houthi movement seized the Yemeni Red Sea port of Mocha and took control
of Yemen's Red Sea coastline — while separately, Iran's Revolutionary Guard reported striking an
unmanned surface vessel at the entrance to the Strait of Hormuz — even as Iranian and Gulf
officials continued a diplomatic track in Oman aimed at defusing the Hormuz standoff.**

**Fact — Houthis advanced materially on the ground.** As of the September 11 daily update (Day
196 of the conflict), Houthi forces took Mocha, a port that commands the approaches to the
Bab-el-Mandeb strait — the choke point through which crude and fuel move from the Gulf to the
Mediterranean via the Suez Canal, and through which Asian and Russian cargo flows the other way
(GlobalSecurity.org). Saudi Arabia's Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman called the US President
twice on September 10 asking for direct strikes on the Houthis; the request was declined, with
the US instead offering intelligence and targeting data (GlobalSecurity.org). Saudi aircraft
struck Mocha's airport twice, per Houthi-affiliated media.

**Fact — separately, at the Strait of Hormuz, Iran reported a strike and the UK flagged an
attack.** The IRGC said it struck an unmanned surface vessel near the Strait of Hormuz entrance;
the UK Maritime Trade Operations authority reported four projectiles hit two vessels roughly four
nautical miles west of Khasab, Oman, with one vessel reported on fire (GlobalSecurity.org).

**Fact — in parallel, Iran and Gulf states continued talking, not just fighting.** Iranian state
media said Tehran would meet Gulf states in Oman to discuss the Strait of Hormuz, and Brent's
Friday pullback to ~$104 from Thursday's $107-109 high was directly attributed to that diplomatic
track (TradingEconomics).

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** the weekend produced escalation on the Red Sea/Bab-el-Mandeb side
(Houthis taking a strategic port, Saudi arms declined by the US) at the same time as
de-escalatory diplomacy on the Hormuz side (Oman talks). **Inference (medium confidence):** oil
markets have been trading almost exclusively off the Hormuz storyline all month (Section 1's
Brent level), but a Houthi-controlled Bab-el-Mandeb is a second, independent chokepoint risk that
hasn't yet been priced the way Hormuz has — if shipping insurers or major carriers start rerouting
around the Red Sea in response to Mocha (rather than Hormuz), that would be a fresh, separate
driver of oil and shipping costs distinct from the Hormuz diplomacy this brief has tracked all
week.

➡️ *What you could do:* Don't extrapolate Friday's oil pullback (Hormuz-driven, de-escalatory) to
the Red Sea side of the conflict — they are two different chokepoints with opposite momentum this
weekend. Watch shipping-insurance and freight-rate headlines specifically for Bab-el-Mandeb/Red

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; the options market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over the
  next 30 days. Below 16 = calm; above 25 = stressed. Frozen at Friday's 15.84 (Section 3).
- **AAII survey** — a weekly poll of individual (retail) investors on bullish/bearish sentiment,
  published Thursdays. This week's reading (38.0% bulls / 39.3% bears) won't update again until
  Thursday, September 17 (Section 3).
- **FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee)** — the Fed's rate-setting body; meets September 15-16,
  with the decision and press conference both Wednesday afternoon, delivered by **Chair Kevin
  Warsh** (sworn in May 22, 2026, succeeding Jerome Powell) — corrected from prior editions
  (Section 1, Section 11).
- **Brent vs. WTI** — Brent is the global oil benchmark (more exposed to Middle East/Strait of
  Hormuz risk); WTI is the US domestic benchmark. Brent settled $104.42-104.47 Friday (Section 1,
  Section 4, Section 7).
- **Bab-el-Mandeb strait** — the Red Sea chokepoint (distinct from the Strait of Hormuz) through
  which shipping between the Gulf/Indian Ocean and the Mediterranean/Suez Canal must pass; the
  Houthi-controlled port of Mocha commands its approaches (Section 4, Section 10).
- **S-1** — the SEC registration statement a company must file before an IPO. Anthropic
  confidentially filed one June 1, 2026; no public version exists yet as of this morning's direct
  EDGAR check, despite reports it would land "shortly after Labor Day" (Section 5, Section 10).
- **Falsifiable version** — this brief's standing discipline for speculative threads: state in
  advance what specific, checkable outcome would confirm or refute the read, then grade it
  honestly once data exists (Section 10).
