**What the primary document actually says:** the only company-specific primary source in this
mix — Western Digital's own most recent quarterly results (filed with the SEC as an 8-K,
reported April 30, 2026) — shows the opposite of a demand warning. Revenue beat estimates
($3.34B vs. $3.23B expected, +45% year-over-year), EPS beat by over 15% ($2.72 vs. $2.36
expected), and the company's own forward guidance for the very quarter now closing called for
**$3.65 billion in revenue (+40% YoY) and gross margin expanding to 51–52%** — an acceleration,
not a slowdown. The company also raised its dividend 20% the same quarter. Nothing in Western
Digital's own filings has revised that guidance downward since.

**The gap:** the selloff was driven by a Korean peer's commentary and a broad "AI memory trade
is overheated" narrative, not by any company-specific data point from the three U.S. names that
actually fell. Micron, SanDisk, and WDC were each up over 200% year-to-date before Friday — the
kind of move where a sentiment-driven pullback is normal, but the coverage framed it as
demand-side news when the only primary document available says the opposite.

💡 *What it means:* **Fact:** this is a framing gap, not a fabricated crisis — nobody claimed
WDC's own numbers were bad, but the headlines let a peer's outlook stand in for company-specific
news without noting that the affected companies' own most recent guidance pointed the other way.
**Inference (low-medium confidence):** this looks more like valuation-driven profit-taking after
a historic run than the start of a fundamental AI-memory-demand story — worth revisiting
specifically when Western Digital next reports (no confirmed August date found in today's
search).

➡️ *What you could do:* Don't read Friday's memory-stock drop as new information about AI
memory demand — the primary document (WDC's own guidance) hasn't changed. Treat it as a
valuation/rotation event until a company in this group actually revises guidance downward,

### Glossary
- **VIX** — the "fear gauge"; expected S&P volatility. Low (<16) = calm; high (>25) = stressed.
- **AAII survey** — weekly poll of retail investors on whether they're bullish/bearish for the next 6 months.
- **PCE deflator (Personal Consumption Expenditures)** — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, distinct from CPI; tracked closely around FOMC meetings.
- **FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee)** — the Fed committee that sets interest rate policy; meets roughly every six weeks.
- **SEP / dot plot** — the Fed's quarterly Summary of Economic Projections, including each official's individual rate forecast; not released at every meeting (not released at this one).
- **ETF proxy** — an exchange-traded fund used to represent an index or asset (SPY for the S&P, GLD for gold); note ETF proxies only trade during US market hours, unlike crypto or oil futures.
- **SOX / SMH** — the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and its tracking ETF (SMH); the standard benchmark for chip-sector performance.
- **8-K** — a filing public companies must make with the SEC to disclose material events, including quarterly earnings press releases; a primary source, unlike a reporter's summary of it.
